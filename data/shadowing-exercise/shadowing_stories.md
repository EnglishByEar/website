# English Shadowing Practice Stories

Read each story aloud, then try shadowing: listen (or read) and repeat right after, matching rhythm and pronunciation. Elementary stories are short and simple; Advanced stories use richer vocabulary and longer sentences.

---

## 🟢 ELEMENTARY (Level 1)

**1. The Red Umbrella**
It was raining hard. Mia did not have an umbrella. Her friend Tom had a big red one. "Come under here," he said. They walked to school together, laughing in the rain.

**2. A New Pet**
Sam wanted a dog. His mom said yes. They went to the shelter. Sam saw a small brown dog. It wagged its tail. "This one," Sam said. He named the dog Buddy.

**3. The Lost Key**
Anna could not find her key. She looked in her bag. She looked under the mat. Finally, she found it in her pocket. "There you are!" she laughed, and opened the door.

**4. Breakfast Time**
Every morning, Leo eats toast and eggs. Today, he made pancakes instead. He added honey on top. His sister smelled the pancakes and ran into the kitchen. "Can I have some too?"

**5. The Bus Stop**
Ben waited at the bus stop. It was cold. He checked his watch. The bus was late. Finally, it arrived. He got on and found a warm seat by the window.

**6. A Rainy Weekend**
It rained all weekend. Kate could not play outside. She read a book instead. Then she baked cookies with her dad. The house smelled sweet. It was a good weekend after all.

**7. The New Student**
A new student joined the class. Her name was Yuki. She was quiet at first. Emma sat next to her and smiled. "Want to be friends?" Emma asked. Yuki nodded happily.

**8. Grandpa's Garden**
Grandpa grows tomatoes in his garden. Every summer, he picks them and shares them with neighbors. Today, he gave a big red tomato to little Jack. "Thank you!" Jack said with a smile.

**9. The Missing Sock**
Ella washed her clothes. After drying them, one sock was missing. She checked everywhere. At last, she found it stuck inside a pillowcase. "Silly sock," she said, and laughed.

**10. A Walk in the Park**
Jake walked his dog in the park. Birds sang in the trees. The dog chased a ball. They sat by the lake and watched the ducks swim. It was a peaceful afternoon.

## 🟡 INTERMEDIATE (Level 2)

**1. The Job Interview**
Sarah was nervous about her interview. She had practiced her answers all week, but her hands still shook as she waited in the lobby. When the manager finally called her name, she took a deep breath and walked in confidently. To her surprise, the conversation felt natural, and she left feeling proud of herself, no matter the outcome.

**2. A Surprise Visit**
Daniel hadn't seen his grandmother in almost a year. One Saturday, he decided to surprise her instead of just calling. He drove three hours without telling her he was coming. When she opened the door and saw him standing there, she burst into tears of joy and hugged him tightly for a full minute.

**3. The Broken Bicycle**
On his way to work, Marcus heard a strange clicking sound coming from his bicycle. He stopped to check it and noticed the chain had come loose. With no tools nearby, he had to walk the rest of the way, arriving fifteen minutes late. Luckily, his boss understood and even helped him fix it during lunch.

**4. Moving to a New City**
When Priya moved to a new city for her job, she knew no one. The first few weeks felt lonely and overwhelming. She joined a local hiking group to meet people with similar interests. By the end of the month, she had made several friends and finally started to feel at home.

**5. The Coffee Shop Regular**
Every morning, an elderly man ordered the same coffee at the same table. The staff learned his name, his order, and even his usual jokes. One day, he didn't show up. The barista worried all day until he finally appeared the next morning, explaining he'd simply overslept.

**6. A Difficult Decision**
Tom had two job offers: one paid more, but the other matched his passion. He spent days weighing the pros and cons, talking to friends and family for advice. In the end, he chose the lower-paying job, believing that doing meaningful work mattered more than a bigger paycheck.

**7. The Storm**
Dark clouds gathered quickly over the coastal town. Fishermen rushed to bring their boats to shore before the storm hit. Within an hour, heavy rain and strong winds swept through the streets. By nightfall, the storm had passed, leaving behind fallen branches and a strange, peaceful silence.

**8. Learning to Cook**
After years of ordering takeout, Rachel decided to learn how to cook. Her first attempts were disasters—burnt rice, undercooked chicken, and a kitchen full of smoke. She didn't give up, though. Six months later, she confidently hosted a dinner party and impressed everyone with a homemade three-course meal.

**9. The Missed Flight**
David arrived at the airport just as his flight was boarding its final passengers. He ran through the terminal, but the gate had already closed. Frustrated, he had to book a new ticket for the next day, but he used the unexpected free time to explore the city instead.

**10. A Small Act of Kindness**
While waiting in line at the grocery store, Maria noticed an elderly woman short on cash. Without hesitation, she offered to pay the difference. The woman thanked her repeatedly, clearly moved. Maria left the store feeling that small gestures could genuinely brighten someone's entire day.

## 🔴 ADVANCED (Level 3)

**1. The Unfinished Manuscript**
For nearly a decade, Professor Whitfield had been quietly working on a novel that no one, not even his closest colleagues, knew existed. When he passed away unexpectedly, his daughter discovered the manuscript tucked inside a weathered leather briefcase in his study. As she read through the pages late into the night, she realized her father had woven fragments of their own family history into the fictional narrative, blurring the line between memoir and invention in a way that left her both heartbroken and profoundly grateful.

**2. Negotiating Under Pressure**
The merger talks had stalled for the third consecutive week, with both legal teams entrenched in seemingly irreconcilable positions over intellectual property rights. Elena, the lead negotiator, sensed that the deal was on the verge of collapsing entirely unless someone was willing to make an unconventional concession. Rather than pushing harder, she proposed a creative compromise that addressed the other side's underlying concerns rather than their stated demands, ultimately salvaging an agreement that had appeared irretrievably lost.

**3. The Archaeological Discovery**
Deep beneath the sun-scorched plateau, the excavation team unearthed a series of ceramic vessels that defied conventional dating methods, suggesting the settlement was centuries older than previously believed. Dr. Alvarez, who had spent her entire career defending an unpopular theory about the region's early trade routes, felt a rush of vindication mixed with disbelief. The discovery would not only reshape academic consensus but also force her to reconsider assumptions she had clung to for the better part of twenty years.

**4. A Crisis of Conscience**
As the whistleblower sat across from the journalist, she weighed the immense personal cost of exposing her company's fraudulent accounting practices against her mounting guilt over staying silent. She had rehearsed this moment countless times in her mind, yet now that it had finally arrived, every scenario she had imagined seemed inadequate. Sliding the folder of evidence across the table, she understood there was no path forward that wouldn't irrevocably alter the course of her life.

**5. The Marathon of Recovery**
Eighteen months after the accident that shattered his leg in three places, Jonah stood at the starting line of the very marathon he had once dominated. Doctors had told him he might never run competitively again, and for a long stretch of his recovery, he had quietly believed them. Crossing the finish line hours slower than his personal best, he felt an unexpected wave of gratitude that had nothing to do with the clock and everything to do with simply being able to run at all.

**6. The Diplomat's Dilemma
Ambassador Chen understood that the peace talks hinged on a single, delicately worded clause that neither delegation was willing to draft first. Any perceived weakness could be exploited, yet excessive rigidity risked derailing negotiations that had taken years to arrange. Late into the night, she drafted seventeen different versions of the same paragraph, ultimately choosing language ambiguous enough to satisfy both sides' immediate political needs while leaving the harder questions for another day.

**7. Rebuilding After the Fire**
When the wildfire swept through the valley, it consumed not only the family's home but also three generations of photographs, letters, and heirlooms that could never be replaced. In the aftermath, Clara found herself oddly unable to mourn the physical loss, focusing instead on the surreal logistics of rebuilding: insurance claims, temporary housing, and the strange grief of relearning where everyday objects belonged. It was months later, while unpacking a single surviving photograph, that the full weight of what had been lost finally hit her.

**8. The Silent Partner**
For years, investors assumed that Marcus alone had built the thriving tech company from nothing, unaware that his college roommate had quietly written the original code that made everything possible. When a journalist uncovered old emails revealing this arrangement, the public narrative unraveled almost overnight. Marcus was forced to publicly acknowledge a contribution he had long minimized, a reckoning that proved as liberating as it was humiliating.

**9. The Last Lecture**
Knowing this would be her final semester before retirement, Professor Okafor abandoned her usual syllabus and instead spent the last class reflecting candidly on the mistakes and unexpected joys of a forty-year academic career. Students who had expected a routine lecture found themselves unexpectedly moved, several admitting afterward that it was the most memorable class they had ever attended. She left the lecture hall that day without fanfare, quietly closing a chapter she had never imagined would end.

**10. The Translator's Burden**
Interpreting for asylum seekers required more than linguistic precision; it demanded an emotional endurance that Farah hadn't fully anticipated when she took the job. Each testimony she translated carried the weight of trauma she could neither fix nor forget, and she often found herself replaying fragmented stories long after her shifts ended. Over time, she learned to hold space for these narratives without being consumed by them, understanding that her role, however small, mattered enormously to those who had no other voice.

---

**Shadowing tip:** Start with Elementary stories at a slow pace, focusing on clear pronunciation of each word. Move to Intermediate once you can repeat sentences smoothly without pausing. Advanced stories are best for practicing natural rhythm, linking sounds, and intonation in longer sentences.
